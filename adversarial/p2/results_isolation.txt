The managed IMAP API now launches a fresh lizard-mail-sync-worker for every
sync, body fetch and IDLE cycle. The parent resolves the endpoint and exactly
one credential, owns the on-disk Tantivy writer and replays committed durable
changes after the worker exits. Requests, responses and errors are bounded;
secrets do not enter argv, environment variables or logs and their serialized
buffers are zeroized.

Linux x86-64 workers require fully enforced Landlock ABI 6, no-new-privileges,
resource ceilings and seccomp. Filesystem access is limited to mail.db,
mail.db-wal, blobs and partial downloads. In particular, the credentials and
search-index trees are not readable. Network creation is limited to INET TCP
streams, and connects are limited to the configured IMAP port and TCP 853.
Execution, ptrace/cross-process memory, kernel keyring, mounts/namespaces,
inbound/Unix/UDP/raw sockets, signaling outside the sandbox and filesystem
ownership/mode/xattr mutation are denied. An unavailable or partially enforced
sandbox aborts before the backend opens; there is no automatic in-process
fallback.

The x86-64 audit architecture also covers x32 ABI syscall numbers. The filter
now rejects `__X32_SYSCALL_BIT` before evaluating its exact native syscall
denylist. A disposable regression process attempts an x32-numbered `getpid`
and must be killed by `SIGSYS`, even if the kernel would otherwise report that
x32 is unsupported.

The explicit residual authority is recorded in spec/imap-sync-isolation.md.
A worker compromise can corrupt or disclose the synchronized profile's mail
database and blobs, and Landlock's network policy is port-based rather than
address-based. The credential vault, other user files and the parent process
are outside the intended authority.
